01 / Foundations

Principles

Five rules that decide every judgement call below. When a choice is ambiguous, the rule wins.

The system holds
  • Everything is a rectangle. Radius is forced to 0 globally. Buttons, images, inputs, avatars and tags are all square-cornered. This is the brand's strongest signature.
  • The hairline is the divider. A 1px warm-black rule at 16% opacity separates content. Boxes and shadows are a last resort.
  • Two typefaces, no more. A light display serif carries voice; a neutral sans carries information.
  • Warmth is non-negotiable. Every neutral is warm-biased. No pure black, no pure white, no cool grey.
  • Space is the luxury. Generous negative space does the work that decoration would otherwise do.
What breaks it
  • Rounded corners or circular crops anywhere, including avatars and icon buttons.
  • A second accent hue. The clay family is the only colour; photography supplies the rest.
  • Drop shadows used for decoration rather than for genuinely floating UI.
  • Body text set in the display serif, or headings set in the sans.
  • Ungraded photography. Mixed stock must pass through the warm grade to read as one world.

23 / Practice

Accessibility contract

Guaranteed
  • Every text pairing in use meets WCAG AA (4.5:1), verified in the matrix above.
  • Focus is always visible: a 2px clay ring, offset 3px, which switches to white on dark grounds.
  • Buttons, chips and icon buttons are at least 44px tall, and 54px at default size. Inline links in dense lists (navigation, footer, breadcrumbs) carry padding to clear the 24px WCAG minimum, and expand to 44px on coarse pointers via @media (pointer:coarse).
  • A skip link precedes the header on every page.
  • Reduced-motion users get the full layout with no animation and no hidden content.
  • Filter state is exposed through aria-pressed; form errors through aria-invalid and aria-describedby.
Watch for
  • Text placed over photography needs its own scrim. The hero gradient exists for this reason.
  • Never use --c-mocha or --c-sand behind small text.
  • Colour alone must not carry meaning; pair it with text or an icon.
  • Decorative images take alt=""; meaningful ones describe the room, not the filename.
